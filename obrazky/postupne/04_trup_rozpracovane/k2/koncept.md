## 1. Joints

### J1 – torso yaw ±150°
* **Actuator:** Feetech STS3215 (7.4 V version, 19.5 kg·cm = 1.91 N·m stall, 12-bit magnetic encoder over 360°,
  45.2 × 24.7 × 35 mm, 55 g; datasheet / vendor listings, confidence high for the footprint, medium for the height –
  I used the larger envelope 35 mm case + 2.6 mm spline boss, plus 11.3 mm for the two 5264 plugs and their bend).
  Inside the torso, shaft **down**, shaft at (−18, 0).
* **Transmission:** 1:1 external spur pair, module 1.0, 18T/18T (pitch Ø18, tip Ø20), centre distance 18.0, face 3.2
  (the stack has exactly 1.0 mm to the hub below and to the torso floor above). The sun gear is fixed to the base hub; the pinion (25T female spline + M3 axial screw) orbits it with the torso, so the
  servo turns exactly by the torso angle (310° of its 360° used). Why off-axis: with the shaft on the axis the servo's
  plug end lands at x ≈ 44.7, outside the soft torso (interior a 42.3 and smaller in the rounded bottom); with the 18 mm
  offset servo + plugs span x −29.5…+27 and stay centred. The pinion/sun pair lives in the 3 mm plinth gap and the base
  top layer, so the servo body starts directly on the torso floor.
* **Bearing:** 6805-2RS 25 × 37 × 7 (C 4.3 kN) – inner ring snapped on the static hub (POM collet on a steel M8 core,
  PETG post on a bridge between the battery groups), outer ring pressed into the rotating dark foot ring r 18.5–20.5. The bearing carries weight and
  tipping moments; the pinion floats axially (0.3 mm) on the spline, so the servo only sees torque.
* **Range / stops:** firmware ±150°; hard stops ±155° = tooth on the foot ring against a block on the hub (below the
  gears, outside the cable channel).
* **Torque:** yaw inertia of torso + head + arms ≈ 6·10⁻⁴ kg·m²; 90° in 0.6 s (triangular profile) → α 17.5 rad/s² →
  0.011 N·m; friction (2RS seals ≈ 0.015, mesh 5 %, rolling loop 0.002) ≈ 0.02 N·m → **0.03 N·m needed vs 1.91 N·m
  stall (≈ 60×)**. Holding: zero (vertical axis).
* **Tooth strength (the K2 weak point):** at full servo stall the m1 tooth would see 212 N (Lewis σ ≈ 220 MPa at 3.2 mm face) – too
  much for POM. Therefore (a) STS3215 torque limit 15 % (0.29 N·m → 32 N, σ ≈ 33 MPa on the 3.2 mm POM pinion, still ≈ 10× the need),
  (b) the brass sun gear sits on a **slip clutch** (wave spring + friction ring, slip 0.25 N·m) so a child twisting the torso
  slips the clutch instead of stripping teeth; the STS3215 load register detects a slip and the firmware re-homes on the
  ±155° stop at low torque, (c) preferred material: brass sun gear + POM pinion (quiet, wear-matched).
* **Backlash:** printed m1 pair 0.08–0.12 mm → 0.5–0.75° of torso yaw (0.6–0.8 mm at the display corner, r 64). Fix:
  slotted servo cradle (±0.3 mm centre distance) set to 0.02–0.03 mm → ≈ 0.2°; option: scissor sun gear (2 × 1.9 mm,
  wire spring). Firmware: approach targets from one side.

### PAN – head yaw ±85° (±90° mechanical)
* **Actuator:** Feetech STS3032 bus servo (360° magnetic encoder, 4.5 kg·cm @ 6 V, ≈ 3.7 kg·cm @ 5 V; vendor listings
  give 23.2 × 12.1 × 28.5 mm, 20–21 g – envelope used 23.5 × 12.5 case, 32.5 tabs, spline top 28.5; confidence medium),
  static in the torso front, shaft up at (0, −21.6).
* **Transmission:** spur m0.8, pinion 18T (Ø14.4) → gear 36T (Ø28.8) on the pan stage, **2:1 reduction**, centre
  distance 21.6, face 3.2/3.5 (POM). Servo ±170° → pan ±85°; resolution 0.044°/step. The gear is toothed only over the
  ~200° that can face the pinion; the remaining back sector is a smooth drum on which the pan rolling loop lies.
* **Bearing:** two-point: bottom = Ø6 steel pin fixed in the deck + bronze sleeve in the gear hub (radial) and a PTFE
  washer under the gear (axial, ≈ 3 N); top = Ø56.4 journal around the well (z 118.5–124.5) in a POM-lined ring of the
  frame (0.05–0.1 mm radial). Bearing span 36 mm → joint play ≤ 0.1/36 rad → ≤ 0.25 mm at the head top.
* **Stops:** pin under the gear in a 180° arc slot of the deck → ±90°; firmware ±85°.
* **Torque:** pan inertia (head + neck + stage) ≈ 2.7·10⁻⁴ kg·m², 85° in 0.35 s → 48 rad/s² → 0.013 N·m; friction
  (PTFE thrust 0.003, journal 0.007, loop 0.002) → 0.026 N·m needed vs 0.72 N·m at the gear (≈ 28×). Tooth: with the
  servo torque limit at 40 % the m0.8 tooth stays < 30 MPa; a 5 N push on the head edge gives only 19 N on the tooth.
* **Backlash:** gear 0.08 mm at r 14.4 (0.32°) + servo internal backlash halved by the 2:1 (≈ 0.25°) → ≈ 0.6° ⇒
  ≈ 0.6 mm at the display corner. Option: a light torsion spring (0.01 N·m) between frame and pan stage to bias one flank.

### LIFT – neck 0…20 mm
* **Actuator:** MG90S (22.8 × 12.2 × 28.5, 13.4 g, 1.8 kg·cm @ 4.8 V / 2.2 @ 6 V; electronics_dims.json) on the pan stage
  **behind the well**, shaft along X at (y 19.1, z 104.8); its lower mounting tab is cut off (as the B neck servo).
* **Mechanism:** crank r 10 (POM) with a Ø2 steel pin in a 2.2 × 12.2 horizontal slot of the **ear** of the neck foot.
  The foot (POM, 45 × 20 × 3) is screwed into the neck bottom (allowed new part) and passes through the collar slot; the
  ear is a separate POM link screwed to the foot through a 4.5 mm window in the well's back wall. lift = 10·(1 − cos θ),
  θ 0 → 180°: **dead centres at rest and at full lift → zero holding torque in both end positions**; the well (47 × 22,
  1 mm clearance, two PTFE tape strips) remains the linear guide.
* **Stops:** crank dead centres; backups: foot 1.0 mm above the well floor, ear 1.4 mm below the journal ring at 20 mm.
* **Torque:** neck + head + foot 213 g → 2.1 N, guide friction (CoG offset y −8 → 0.4 N) + loop 0.1 N → 2.6 N ×
  r 10 = 0.026 N·m max (mid-stroke) vs 0.177 N·m stall @ 5 V → **6.8×**. Holding at the awake pose (12.5 mm, θ 104.5°):
  0.025 N·m (≈ 15 % of stall, acceptable; dead centres are used for rest and full lift). Full stroke 0.36 s at 5 V
  (firmware ramps to ≈ 0.5 s).

### TILT – unchanged (variant B rev. E2)
MG90S in the neck, tilt-vs-lift interlock table of `B/montaz.txt`; PWM and 5 V from the torso controller through the
pan loop. Verified here with the **real head mesh** against the K2 placeholder top over the whole table and pan
−85…85: min 0.45 mm at the collar plateau edge (r 29.8, same B criterion ≥ 0.25), ≥ 1 mm elsewhere; real hinge lugs vs
the K2 well: 1.0 mm (`head_real_check.json`).

### ARMS – one servo inside each paddle
* **Actuator:** slim metal-gear micro servo, EMAX ES9052MD class (listings: 19.9 × 8.8 × 23.1–26.35 mm, 5.5–6 g,
  1.3 kg·cm @ 6 V; envelope used 21 case + 5.35 boss/spline along X, 20.5 × 9.4 – larger listing, confidence medium).
  The case is fixed in the paddle and turns with the arm; its spline sits in a **static spline socket** on the side
  column of the torso frame (r 7 hub at |x| 37.5–43). The arm pulls off sideways (magnetic detent) for service.
* Why in the paddle: inside the torso the shoulder servos would sit exactly in the pan-stage sweep (r ≤ 35.9 around the
  well); in the paddle they cost only 2–3 mm of paddle thickness.
* **Range:** −45…+135° (180° servo travel, verify 500–2500 µs on purchase). The head (|x| 55, ears 58.8) is wider than
  the arm's inner face (|x| 46.5), so the upper limit depends on the head pose – firmware table `check.json →
  arm_limit_table_deg` (every entry verified with ≥ 2 mm to the head envelope). Right arm at pan 0: 102° (rest), 125°
  (lift 20), 95–98° while the head nods down (lift 12.5/+30°, lift 20/+40°), 144–151° while it looks up; with the head
  panned ≥ 60° and not nodding towards that arm the geometry allows 180°, i.e. the full 135° servo travel. Left arm =
  same table with the pan sign inverted. The firmware clamps the arm command to this table (interpolated, −2° margin).
* **Torque:** paddle 10.6 g with CoG 25 mm below the axis + servo on the axis → 0.003 N·m holding at 90°, 0.0014 N·m to
  accelerate 90° in 0.25 s → 0.0045 N·m vs 0.108 N·m @ 5 V (≈ 24×).
* Clearances: paddle ↔ torso 2.0 mm, black cap ↔ shoulder pocket 1.0 mm, paddle ↔ base ≥ 5.0 mm (tip at z 45 over the
  base top z 40, also over J1 ±150°), the two arms never meet.

### WHEELS – differential drive
* 2 × N20 6 V 1:150 with magnetic encoder (12 × 10 × 34 + encoder 8 + plug 5; 7 PPR × 4 × 150 = 4200 counts/wheel
  rev), wheels Ø32 × 9 (TPU tyre) at x ±54, track 108, axle z 16, 6 mm below the base bottom; DRV8833 (Pololu
  20.3 × 15.2). Two 3/8″ ball casters at y ±34 (support diamond). Ground clearance 6 mm.
* **Torque:** robot ≈ 0.89 kg: rolling 0.2 N + 0.5 m/s² + 5° ramp 0.85 N → 1.55 N → 0.012 N·m per wheel vs
  ≈ 0.2 N·m stall (≈ 16×). Speed ≈ 110 rpm → 0.18 m/s at 6 V (desk use 0.05–0.12 m/s).

## 2. Cable routing

**J1 (6 conductors, ±155°):** VBAT ×2, GND ×2, servo/node bus, wake/spare. Default: custom polyimide FPC 6.0 × 0.15 mm
(2 oz Cu: VBAT/GND traces 1.3 mm, bus/wake 0.4 mm; ≈ 4 A peak total); alternative: 4 × AWG26 + 2 × AWG30 silicone
Kapton-bonded on edge (6.4 mm). Path: base charger/BMS area (back) → static anchor on the outer wall of the channel
(inner face r 32.5, ≈ 120°) → outer run on the static wall → U-turn → inner run on the rotating foot ring (r 20.5) → rotating anchor →
up through the torso floor at r ≈ 20 → JST-GH 6p on the torso controller. Rolling loop in the annulus r 21.5–34,
z 30.6–37.2: U-turn centreline radius ≈ 5.3 mm (≈ 35× FPC thickness), U-turn travel 0.40 × Δθ (125° for 310°),
**loop length in the channel ≈ 103 mm** (+ 2 × 15 mm leads → 135 mm). No slip ring, no twist.

**PAN (8 conductors, ±85° + 20 mm lift):** head 4 (5 V_H, GND_H AWG28 PTFE Ø0.85; TX, RX AWG30) + servo group 4
(5 V_S, GND_S AWG28; PWM tilt, PWM lift AWG30) as a 2 × 4 Kapton-bonded ribbon 3.6 × 1.8 mm. Path: head → neck
channels (B) → neck bottom exit (x 13.8) → channel inside the POM foot → foot +X end → window in the well's +X end wall →
**vertical lift loop** on the outside of that wall (x 25.3–27.8, z 89.5–113; U-turn rises 10 mm for 20 mm lift) → down
under the well → rotating anchor on the smooth drum sector of the pan gear → inner run on the drum (r 15.7) → U-turn
(centreline R 5.2) → outer run on the static back-half wall (r 26.1) → static anchor ≈ 20° → through the deck to the
controller (JST-SH 4p + 4p). Loop in the channel ≈ 56 mm; U-turn travel 0.37 × Δθ (63° for 170°); everything stays in the
back half, away from the pan pinion at the front. Lift-servo leads (3) join the ribbon on the pan stage via a 2 × 3 pin
micro-junction (shared 5 V_S/GND_S).

**Arms:** 3 × AWG30 per arm, one-turn flat spiral in the shoulder pocket (r 7.5–12.5, |x| 41.2–43.2), JST-SH 3p inside
the torso. Range 180° → spiral rotates ±0.25 turn.

* The base node (RP2040-Zero, 23.5 × 18) is bus ID 20: wheel speeds, encoder counts (PIO quadrature), cliff stop
  locally within 1 ms, ToF, charger status, LED bar. Battery voltage is read from the STS3215 voltage register.
* **5 V rail (torso):** typical 0.55 A; firmware-capped peak 2.5 A (max. two micro-servos in fast motion at once, audio
  peaks buffered by 470 µF) < 2.8 A of the D36V28F5.
* **Charging:** IP2326 boost charger (5 V/2 A in, 8.4 V CC/CV ≈ 1 A, ≈ 6–7 h for 6 Ah). The robot may stay on while
  charging (USB 10 W > 2.8 W idle), but the charger only terminates when the robot is off or asleep (< 50 mA); a full
  charge is therefore done with the main switch off. Charge state shown on the base LED bar + a small LED at the port.
  (True power-path is a phase-2 option: buck-boost NVDC charger.)

## 5. Assembly order, service, hidden fasteners
**Base:** (1) heat-set inserts in the white top shell; (2) chassis (PETG) with motor clamps, hub bridge, J1 hub
(steel M8 core) + slip-clutch sun gear, J1 channel with the FPC static anchor; (3) motors + encoders + wheels; (4) PCBs:
node, DRV8833, buck (corner pockets beside the battery bays), BMS, charger + USB-C + switch (on top of the battery
groups, outside r 35), ToF + LED bar at the front; (5) dark bottom plate with casters and cliff boards; (6) the two
battery groups (series link + balance lead, XT30 + JST-XH, no hot glue) drop into two bays from below, each closed by
a cover with 2 × M2 – **the only screws of the base, all on the underside**.
**Torso:** (1) printed frame = cradle + deck + 4 columns + top journal ring (2 parts, screwed); (2) STS3215 into the
cradle (4 × M2 side holes), pinion; (3) STS3032 + pinion (2 tab screws); (4) pan stage: pivot pin, PTFE washer, pan gear
(stop pin), well, lift MG90S + crank; (5) controller, buck, amp, cap, speaker, LED bar, arm spline sockets + cable
spirals; (6) shell = rear shell + front panel (R01 front-panel seams): rear shell screwed from the torso bottom (inside
the plinth gap, invisible), front panel on 4 N52 magnets + 2 locating pins; (7) collar ring glued in; (8) neck + head
from above per B montaz: neck + foot pass through the collar slot, then the ear link is screwed to the foot through
the back-wall window (rear shell off), collar disc on; (9) torso onto the base: the 6805 (pressed into the foot ring)
slides over the sun gear (Ø20 < bore Ø25) and snaps over a 3-finger POM collet lip of the hub (pull-off ≈ 30 N, 8× the
weight of the base, so the robot can be lifted by the torso), the pinion meshes vertically, FPC plugged through the
rear opening.
**Service:** battery – bottom covers; USB-C/switch – on the back of the base; arm servo – pull the arm off, unplug
JST-SH; lift servo, controller, pan loop – rear shell off (screws under the torso); pan servo – front panel off;
STS3215 – pull the torso off the collet, unplug the FPC (15 mm slack), 4 screws. No screws on top/front/sides.

## 6. How much the soft shell constrains K2
The minimum interior that encloses all K2 internals + 1.0 mm (`envelope.json`, slices every 5 mm, superellipse with the
placeholder aspect b/a = 0.9) is **a ≥ 40.9 mm** for n 2.35–3.0 (41.2 for n 2.2), set by the pan/lift sweep at
z 88–118 and by the J1-servo zone at z 53–73 → with a 2.2 mm wall the smallest soft torso is **≈ 86 × 78 mm**; R01's
91 × 78 fits. The placeholder is 89 × 80 because parts that are screwed to the shell anyway (side columns with the arm
spline sockets at |x| ≤ 41.5, speaker at |y| ≤ 36.6) sit right at the wall. The base needs a ≥ 68.0 (n 2.35,
b/a 0.775) → **≥ 140.4 × 109.8 mm**; the exponent barely matters (68.1 at n 2.2) because the critical points are the
wheels (|x| 59.5), the ToF window and the battery-pack ends, not the corners.

What the soft shell actually cost:
1. **J1 servo off the axis.** With the STS3215 shaft on the axis its plug end (+11 mm) lands at x ≈ 44.7, outside the
   rounded interior (42.3, less in the R16 bottom). The 18 mm offset + 1:1 spur pair keeps servo and plugs within
   x −29.5…+27 – the main reason for K2's gears.
2. **Depth is set by the lift crank:** the crank/ear sweep r 35.9 about Z (back and right sector) needs an interior
   depth ≥ 2 × 36.9 → 78.2 mm outside with a 2.2 wall (placeholder 80).
3. **Bottom rounding (R16):** below z 53 only the centred STS3215 fits; the amp, buck and controller moved up to
   z ≥ 52–55, the pan servo into the front band (y −15…−28) where the superellipse still has full depth.
4. **Top:** a 1 mm collar plateau r 29.8 is needed in every direction, because pan ±85° swings the nodding head over the
   wide X sides where a plain R16 edge stays too high (real-head check: 0.45 mm over the plateau, ≥ 1 mm beyond). The
   arm sockets (axis 21 mm below the top) sit just under the R16 top edge, so the shoulder pockets are partly cut into
   the rounded shoulder – the R01 look of caps on the upper corners.
5. **Base corners:** the four 66.5 mm cells end 1–2 mm from the n 2.35 / 142 × 110 interior; the four small boards
   (node, driver, buck, charger) live in the triangular pockets beside the cell ends.
6. **Plinth:** with ≥ R15 edges the torso's flat bottom is only ≈ 57 × 48, so the rotating dark skirt r 34 shows under
   the rounded belly as the R01 plinth ring – a feature, but it fixes the base top as flat to r 36.

## 7. Top risks
1. **J1 gear mesh is the weakest link** against a user twisting the torso → slip clutch + torque limit + re-homing are
   mandatory, brass sun gear recommended.
2. **Yaw backlash** of two gear pairs (J1 + pan) ≈ 0.2–0.8° each → visible head wobble of 0.3–0.8 mm if not adjusted.
3. **Zero vertical slack** in the torso stack (STS3215 top 80.5 → deck 81). The STS3215 height is taken as the larger
   envelope (35 + 2.6); if the real servo is 35 overall we gain 2.6 mm.
4. **Pan loop on a half-toothed drum** is novel; fallback = loose 8-wire S-loop behind the well (needs ≈ 8 mm more torso
   height).
5. **Arm/head interference** is inherent to R01 proportions → firmware table; the slim arm servo (8.8 mm class) must be
   verified for 180° travel and availability; with an MG90S the paddle grows to ≈ 26 mm.
6. **Lift holds torque away from the dead centres** (awake pose 12.5 mm): MG90S hum; consider dead-centre-only poses or a
   bus servo (SCS0009) for silent holding.
7. **Base corners:** the battery groups end 1–2 mm from the placeholder base wall at the four corners – the exterior
   must keep ≥ 142 × 110 at z 11–30 with n ≥ 2.3 or the base grows.
8. **Head top clearance in X:** the wide torso (89) must still drop ≥ 1 mm beyond r 30 in every direction (pan ±85 swings
   the nodding head over the X sides) – collar plateau r 29.8 + 1 mm step in the placeholder.
